# Planning notes: Measure Twice

Why this idea: we build Stone Project, a marketplace where countertop fabricators list stone remnants, so the people we talk to every week are fabricators. The step they double-check most is the sink cutout layout: a paper template, a tape measure and a pencil, checked again and again because a cutout in the wrong place scraps the piece. It is a moment with both hands busy and the eyes on the stone, which is what glasses are for.

This is a new project for Round 2 (started 17 Nov 2026). Our Round 1 entry, Remnant Fit, was for a homeowner checking whether a remnant covers a vanity. Measure Twice is for the fabricator at the shop table, and shares no code with it.

Decisions taken while planning with the plan-lens skill:
- One spatial moment: the cutout outline on the slab, with the distances written on the stone. A full kitchen top was dropped because it never fits in the 37 x 32 degree view; a 122 x 56 cm vanity blank at 0.8 m almost does, and the arrow-and-tick hint covers the corner that falls outside.
- Numbers live on the stone, between the outline and each edge, where the tape would be. No floating panel.
- The slab is a stand-in with a "Slab is here" button, because the preview can't detect a real surface.
- "Marked" is a button, not voice, so it can be tried with the mouse and tested with LEAF. Voice is listed as what the real version needs.
- The thinnest allowed rail is a slider, not a fixed rule, because each shop has its own number. 7.5 cm is only the starting value.
- Light stone and a cyan line: the display is additive, so dark stone and dark lines would vanish.
- The Lens stops at pencil marks. No cutting guidance.
